# How our earlier literature review links to the report

Notes updated: 27 October 2026 (Perth).

I compared our 16-page `Literature Review (Cryptanalysis).pdf` with our report draft of 26 October. The task sheet describes the earlier review as August work. Its reference list on PDF pages 15-16 has **15 sources**. The report version I checked has **26**, numbered in [my reference audit](REFERENCE_AUDIT.md).

I wanted to see which papers we kept, which ones we left out, and how the project changed. This comparison uses the report we have now; it will need updating if the reference list changes.

We kept five of the earlier fifteen sources and left out ten. That means twenty-one of the report's twenty-six references were added after the earlier review. They cover classical attacks, the ciphers themselves, search costs, enumeration, statistics and benchmarking. Leaving out a source does not mean that its findings were wrong; it may simply be less relevant to the shorter report.

## How our question changed

The earlier review supported the idea of using confidence to decide when to trust neural guidance. Our [timeline overview](../../timeline/README.md) and [confidence-gate notes](../../timeline/03_confidence_gate_and_first_comparison.md) show that this did not give a reliable advantage. The report now asks when ranking saves enough search time to repay its own cost.

I would describe confidence gating as an idea we tested with negative results. I would not list it as a successful final contribution. The review also covered methods we did not implement. I have kept those papers separate from the baselines we actually measured, and used the current report's terms in my background notes and glossary.
