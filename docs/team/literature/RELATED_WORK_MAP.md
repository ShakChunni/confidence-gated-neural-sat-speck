# How the closest papers relate to our work

Notes updated: 27 October 2026 (Perth).

I chose these ten papers because they are close to our ranking, recovery or solver work. I have compared what they measure, the ciphers they use, and where our study differs. I am not using the table to rank the strongest published attacks.

The reference numbers link to [my audit](REFERENCE_AUDIT.md). I kept distinguisher rounds separate from complete-attack rounds. Data budgets, hardware and success rules also differ, so the headline results are not directly comparable.

| Paper / primary source | What it measures | Cipher / target | Round scope relevant to comparison | How this project differs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gohr 2019, audit 13 ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-030-26951-7_6)) | Distinguishing, correct-key rank and neural-aided recovery cost | SPECK32/64 | Nine-round ranking example; 11-round attack; shorter-round distinguishers | We use published rankers in seven/eight-round pipelines and count preparation, completion and verification within the stated clock. |
| Bao et al. 2022, audit 4 ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-22963-3_11)) | Neural/classical distinguishing and key-recovery attack cost | SPECK32/64; SIMON32/64 | Improved 12- and practical 13-round SPECK attacks; our report attributes SIMON VV9/VV8 and a 16-round attack to this work | Our ten-round SIMON comparison uses its published models. We are not claiming to reproduce its strongest attacks. |
| Zhang, Wang, and Wang 2024, audit 24 ([IACR](https://cic.iacr.org/p/1/3/13)) | Accuracy, inference cost, compression and key recovery | SPECK32/64; SIMON32/64 | Nine-round SPECK and 12-round SIMON distinguishers; 13/14-round SPECK and 17-round SIMON attacks | We test verified recovery at lower round counts under matched local budgets, rather than aiming for a round record. |
| Benamira et al. 2021, audit 7 ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-030-77870-5_28)) | Learned features, interpretability and non-neural distinguishing alternatives | Primarily reduced-round SPECK32/64 | Multiple reduced-round distinguisher settings; no single recovery round count describes this comparison | Our endpoint is a checked master key after ranking. I use this paper to explain why the classical comparison needs to be strong. |
| Biryukov et al. 2023, audit 9 ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-33488-7_6)) | Classical recovery and time-data trade-offs | SPECK family | Experimentally verified 11-round SPECK32 recovery; broader analysis extends to other variants/depths | Our seven/eight-round timing experiments use much less chosen-plaintext data. I would not convert this paper's complexity exponents into local wall time. |
| Andrzejczak and Dudzic 2019, audit 2 ([publisher PDF](https://www.infocommunications.hu/documents/169298/4647244/InfocomJ_2019_4_1_Andrzejczak.pdf)) | SAT attack time and validity of returned keys with/without the key schedule | Reduced-round SIMON and SPECK | Several round/pair settings; Section IV reports failure at six-round SPECK and eight-round SIMON within one hour with schedule equations | We keep the key schedule and check the master key independently. Unrelated round-key assignments are not the same result. |
| Soos, Nohl, and Castelluccia 2009, audit 21 ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-642-02777-2_24)) | Exact cryptographic solving under representation/solver changes | Crypto-1, HiTag2, Bivium stream ciphers | Block-cipher round count is not applicable | This gives the XOR-aware solver background. We measure how ranked block-cipher candidates and solver settings change rejection cost. |
| Zheng et al. 2024, audit 25 ([NeurIPS](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/494f876fad056843f310ad647274dd99-Abstract-Conference.html)) | Satisfiability-classification accuracy and key-inference accuracy | Cryptographic ANF/SAT benchmark instances | Multiple generated sizes/configurations, not one common attack round count | We require a verified master key. Classification speed and accuracy answer a different question. |
| Zheng et al. 2025, audit 26 ([NeurIPS](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/69d97a6493fbf016fff0a751f253ad18-Abstract-Datasets_and_Benchmarks_Track.html)) | Benchmarking, learned guidance, hyperparameter optimization and BASIN | SAT4CryptoBench cipher/hash instance families | Multiple round reductions; official implementation includes SIMON-labelled 10-12-round datasets | We focus on two cipher variants, paired key sets and a stated online budget. Our acceptance rule is kept explicit. |
| Anonymous enumerative SAT manuscript 2026, audit 19 ([OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=l7GZ3vswuD)) | Neural selection/enumeration with classical solvers and an ANF-based recovery framework | Cryptographic SAT benchmarks including SIMON examples in the earlier review | Multi-configuration benchmark; exact configuration claims not reverified because direct primary access was challenged | This combination already exists in earlier work. I would claim our controlled cost comparison and fresh-key tests, not the first neural-plus-SAT recovery. The current acceptance status still needs checking. |

The survey in reference 12 and the classical papers in references 1, 5 and 8 give more background beyond these ten. Where a paper studies several settings, I have left that clear instead of assigning it one round count. The access limits in the table still apply if we reuse it for the presentation.

What sets our study apart is the measured link between candidate order, cost and verified completion within a budget. That is the claim I would keep. It applies to our tested cohorts, hardware and solver settings, rather than full-round security or a general advantage for neural ranking.
